# Interview Preparation Guide

## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility),
**Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do
differently.

## Common Tough Questions

Fill in your own honest draft answers below — these are prompts for what to think through, not text to
recite verbatim.

### "Why are you making this career move?" (or "why did you leave your last role")
> [Draft an honest, specific answer. Frame as a deliberate, planned transition where possible, not an
> escape from something negative.]

### "You don't have [X] experience that this role wants."
> [Acknowledge the gap honestly. Bridge with the closest real experience you have. Don't claim more scale
> or depth than your CV actually supports.]

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> [Draft a default answer tied to your actual career goals from `job-evaluation.md`. Adjust per the role's
> actual growth path once known.]

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> [Draft something you genuinely recognize in yourself, plus a concrete step you've taken to counter it.
> Avoid the cliché "I work too hard" non-answer.]

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Must reference specific projects, values, or market position from that company's
> actual research/website — never generic. Verify claims per `writing-style.md` rule 5
> before using them in an actual interview answer, same bar as the cover letter.

## Questions to Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between new projects and maintenance work?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file).
- Keep water nearby.
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague.
- It's OK to take a few seconds to think before answering.
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Interview / Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** post-submission — risks a negative impression.
- If a timeline was given, respect it and wait.
- If no timeline and 2+ weeks have passed, a brief status check is acceptable.
- A short follow-up is fine if there's genuinely new, relevant information to share.

### Thank-You / Response Notes
- On any update (interview invite, rejection, status update), send a brief, warm
  reply — see `Application Materials/Rejection_Response.md` for a starting tone/format
  you can adapt.
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences).

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate.
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself").
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions.
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using competencies from the actual posting.
5. End with a tough question or curveball.
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen.
7. Suggest which STAR example above fits best for each question.
